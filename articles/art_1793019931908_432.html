Aleksandar Vučić Concedes Defeat to Student-Led Movement in Historic Serbian Election

Serbia's long-standing leader Aleksandar Vučić has conceded defeat in snap parliamentary elections, handing a monumental victory to a student-led opposition movement that capitalised on widespread public anger against state corruption.

Preliminary figures from the state election commission, with 96 per cent of polling stations reporting, showed the insurgent movement securing 52.65 per cent of the vote against 39.2 per cent for the ruling Serbian Progressive Party (SNS). The result marks a dramatic conclusion to the SNS's twelve-year grip on power, during which Vučić systematically consolidated control over state institutions and the domestic media landscape.

Jubilant crowds flooded the streets of Belgrade, Novi Sad, and other regional centres in the early hours of Monday morning. Demonstrators waved national flags, set off fireworks, and danced to celebratory chants after an election that had drawn long queues at polling stations domestically and across the Serbian diaspora in cities including Munich, Paris, and Chicago.

The opposition movement was forged less than two years ago following a disaster at Novi Sad railway station, where the collapse of a newly renovated concrete canopy killed 16 people. The tragedy galvanised mass protests against systemic negligence, cronyism, and unregulated public procurement contracts. Rather than remaining an insular student body, the coalition assembled a broad civic front, fielding candidates from across society, including university academics, medical doctors, cultural figures, and sports professionals.

Vučić, who had reached his constitutional two-term limit as president, triggered the snap parliamentary election with the aim of returning to the prime ministership—an office he previously occupied until 2017. Admitting defeat at party headquarters in Belgrade, he acknowledged the definitive nature of the vote, stating simply: "It is clear we have lost these elections."

International reaction followed swiftly. European Commissioner for Enlargement Marta Kos welcomed the development, remarking that "a new chapter can begin for Serbia" and reaffirming that the path towards European Union membership remains accessible. In contrast, the Kremlin struck a cautious tone. Russian government spokesperson Dmitry Peskov commended Vučić's record on bilateral ties whilst noting that future cooperation with the incoming administration in Belgrade would depend on whether both parties sought common ground.

Attention will now turn to the formal designation of a prime minister from within the civic coalition, alongside preparations for a presidential election scheduled for December. Parliament speaker Ana Brnabić is presently fulfilling the head-of-state duties in a caretaker capacity.
