British Mother Dies Heroically Rescuing Sons from Bahamas Riptide During Anniversary Holiday

A British holidaymaker has lost her life whilst saving her two young sons after they were swept into open water by a suspected riptide in the Bahamas. Leanne Gregory, 36, from Stapleford in Nottinghamshire, had been visiting the Caribbean with her husband Nathan to celebrate an early milestone ahead of their tenth wedding anniversary in December.

The incident took place off the coast of Bimini on Tuesday morning. Emergency responders and bystanders managed to pull the two boys safely from the water, but Mrs Gregory was recovered unresponsive at the North Bimini Ferry Dock shortly after 10:00 local time. Despite immediate attempts at cardiopulmonary resuscitation and transport to a nearby medical clinic, she was pronounced dead. The Royal Bahamas Police Force confirmed an autopsy will be conducted, noting that foul play is not suspected.

Her husband, who has now returned home to Nottinghamshire with their two sons, paid an emotional tribute to his partner of 22 years. The couple first met during infant school and began dating at the age of 14. Describing his wife as the 'foundation' of their household, he expressed profound gratitude for her final act of devotion, hailing her as a hero who gave everything to ensure their children survived.

Mr Gregory also expressed appreciation to airline and cruise operators who assisted the family during their harrowing return journey. Back in Stapleford, the local community has rallied around the grieving father and sons, establishing an online fundraiser that swiftly amassed thousands of pounds to ease their immediate financial and logistical burdens.

Local civic leaders offered their condolences, pointing to the close-knit character of the town and pledging continued support for the family during their bereavement. The Foreign, Commonwealth & Development Office stated that it remains in close contact with Bahamian authorities to provide consular assistance to the family.
