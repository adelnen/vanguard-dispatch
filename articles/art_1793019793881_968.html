NHS Consultant Doctors in England to Stage Two-Day Strike Over Pay and Working Hours

Senior hospital doctors across England are set to stage a two-day walkout next month following the breakdown of pay negotiations with the government. The British Medical Association announced that the 48-hour strike will commence at 06:59 GMT on 25 November, with consultants providing only emergency-level staffing across NHS trusts.

The adoption of so-called 'Christmas Day cover' means routine, planned, and elective hospital procedures will face severe disruption, leaving tens of thousands of scheduled operations and consultations facing postponement. The decision follows months of behind-the-scenes talks that failed to resolve grievances stemming from a ballot in July, when 76% of participating BMA members backed industrial action.

While NHS consultants received a 3.5% pay rise this year, the trade union maintains that real-terms earnings have plummeted by roughly a quarter over the past 17 years once inflation is accounted for. Beyond baseline remuneration, the union is pressing for a reduction in the standard working week from 40 to 35 hours, enhanced premiums for unsocial hours, and reformed pension rules that afford clinicians greater retirement flexibility.

Dr Helen Neary and Dr Shanu Datta, co-chairs of the BMA consultants committee, insisted ministers had left the medical workforce with no viable alternative. In a joint statement, the representatives noted that while withdrawal of labour does not come naturally to senior clinicians, repeated delays from Whitehall had eroded trust, leaving the profession feeling undervalued after years of sustained operational strain.

Government officials, however, have strongly criticised the planned walkout. Health Secretary Yvette Cooper expressed disappointment that dates had been set amidst ongoing discussions, describing the strike as irresponsible and cautioning that the disruption would place an already overstretched health service under acute pressure. Departmental figures suggest that meeting the BMA's collective demands would incur a financial commitment of between £2 billion and £3 billion per year.

The impending industrial action in England coincides with planned stoppages by doctors in Northern Ireland next month. It also revives an era of industrial friction within the NHS that many had hoped had drawn to a close following the resolution of resident doctors' prolonged disputes earlier this year.
