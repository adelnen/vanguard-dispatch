High Court Awards Carer £127,000 from Mother's Estate in Landmark Ruling

A woman has spoken of her immense relief after securing a landmark High Court victory entitling her to £127,500 for the full-time care she provided to her elderly mother during the final years of her life.

Bern Rogers, from Bristol, endured a protracted and bitter family dispute following the death of her mother, Sheila, in 2020. The conflict saw Ms Rogers initially subjected to criminal proceedings after transferring £100,000 from her late mother’s account to cover the cost of her care. She was subsequently acquitted of theft and fraud charges by a Bristol Crown Court jury in 2022 before pursuing the matter through the civil courts.

In a consequential ruling, the High Court determined that Ms Rogers had entered into an informal but legally binding contract with her mother, who suffered from dementia and severe physical ailments, for reasonable remuneration. A judge later calculated the sum owed at £127,500, reflecting 850 days of round-the-clock domestic care that avoided the necessity of costly institutional placement.

Ms Rogers explained that her mother had moved into her home in September 2017 after becoming increasingly frail and disoriented. Caring for her rapidly evolved into a continuous undertaking, preventing Ms Rogers and her husband, Jon, from leaving the house unaccompanied. During her mother's final weeks, Ms Rogers slept on the floor of her bedroom to provide vigilant oversight. Whilst she maintained she would have cared for her mother regardless, she noted that Sheila had expressly insisted that her daughter be properly compensated for her labour.

Legal authorities have hailed the decision as a critical indicator of how the judiciary views domestic care duties. Specialists noted that the courts demonstrated a clear readiness to apply commercial contractual principles to domestic relationships. The ruling acknowledges the totality of caregiving—including continuous supervision and personal curtailment—even where an exact monetary figure was not formally committed to writing.

The executor of the estate, Ms Rogers’ brother Andrew Wills, maintained that the siblings were aware only of an arrangement to provide £50 per week for upkeep, alongside genuine expenses. Whilst the determination establishing the existence of a valid contract cannot be appealed, the estate has indicated its intention to lodge an appeal against the specific financial sum awarded.
